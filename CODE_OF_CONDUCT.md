# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith until someone gives you a reason not to. Criticise code, not people.

## What is expected

- **Disagree with evidence.** Technical disagreement is welcome; it's how the numbers in this repo got honest.
  "This misses callers reached through a fixture, here's a repro" is useful. "This is wrong" is not.
- **Be concrete.** A bug report with the command, the repo and the output is worth ten describing a feeling.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** Graph queries, tree-sitter and call resolution are a lot to take in at once. Point
  people to the right file instead of telling them they should already know.
- **Accept a decision once it's made.** Raise a concern with evidence. If it's heard and the call goes the other
  way, it's recorded in `DECISIONS.md` and the project moves on. A new fact reopens it; repetition doesn't.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in public or in private.
- Discriminatory language or imagery of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews or issues.
- Sexualised language or attention.

## Scope

This applies everywhere the project lives: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and anywhere someone is
representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/TheManishCode/aftershocks---Graph-Hacks/security/advisories/new)
(it's the private channel GitHub offers; use it for conduct reports too) or by contacting
[@TheManishCode](https://github.com/TheManishCode) directly. Reports are kept confidential.

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, commits, issues and other contributions that break this code, and may
temporarily or permanently ban anyone whose behaviour is harmful. Responses scale with the harm: a note, then a
warning, then a ban.
